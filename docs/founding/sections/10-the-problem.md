**Settled**{.badge .settled} · You can't see an outfit until you're already wearing it.

Trying on is expensive — physically (digging the pieces out, putting them on, taking them off) and mentally (holding the combination in your head). So people wear the same five outfits and forget the other forty hang in the closet.

**Settled**{.badge .settled} · Existing closet apps stop at cataloguing.

They photograph each garment on a hanger or against a wall and call it digitized. You end up with a spreadsheet of clothes, not a mirror. The thing nobody ships is the *try-on* — the moment you see the pieces on your own body, layered, and judge it in a glance.

**Leaning**{.badge .leaning} · The hard part is the base image, not the catalogue.

A garment shot is easy. A *consistent, well-lit, background-clean image of the person* — the canvas everything else hangs on — is where every naive attempt falls apart. If the base is bad, no amount of overlay quality saves the result.
